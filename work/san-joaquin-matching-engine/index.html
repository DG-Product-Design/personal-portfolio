San Joaquin Matching Engine

Helping social workers find prospective adoptive families

At Dito, I designed and built a tool that helped San Joaquin County social workers compare prospective adoptive families for children awaiting adoption. The matching engine used weighted criteria to rank potential matches for review.

Project role
Lead UX Designer & Lead UI Developer
Client
San Joaquin County
Recognition
2017 CCISDA Innovation Award
Scope
I led the UX design and frontend development of a Section 508-compliant matching application. I interviewed stakeholders, prototyped the workflow, designed the interactions, and built the production interface around the county's operating requirements.

Product-engineering contribution

  • UX + UI design lead
  • Accessibility design
  • Product engineering lead

Technologies

  • Adobe XD
  • Angular

  • JavaScript
  • TypeScript
  • Google Cloud

Key decisions

  1. 01Turn the county's matching criteria into weighted rankings without taking the final decision away from social workers.
  2. 02Show ranked results beside family profiles so workers can see the information behind a potential match.
  3. 03Meet Section 508 requirements and move the frontend to TypeScript so the application is easier to maintain.
  1. SelectStart with a child's profile

    Choose the profile to compare against prospective families.

  2. MatchApply weighted criteria

    The engine scores and ranks potential matches.

  3. ReviewInspect potential matches

    Social workers can open family profiles alongside the ranked results.

Bring the comparison into one workflow

The matching engine was part of the county's I-CAN initiative. Social workers compared a child's profile with prospective families using several criteria. We put ranked results and profile details in the same workflow so workers could see why a family appeared as a potential match.

Design and build the interface

I designed the workflow in Adobe XD and built the interface in Angular with Material Design. The UI met Section 508 accessibility requirements. We also moved the frontend from JavaScript to TypeScript and deployed it to Google App Engine through GitLab CI/CD.

Project recognition

The project received a 2017 Innovation Award from the California County Information Services Directors Association (CCISDA).
